**Takeaway:** Europe buys layered systems from primes (Kongsberg, Rheinmetall, Thales, MBDA) but is placing interceptor-drone orders with young domestic firms (TYTAN, Origin, Cambridge Aerospace, Frankenburg). The EU drone-defence initiative aims at [initial capability by end-2026](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2025/10/15/eu-plans-functional-drone-wall-against-russia-by-end-of-2027-leak-shows) and the SAFE loan instrument can finance purchases. That favors European suppliers.

- **Brave1 Market:** 590,000+ UAVs ordered in January–September 2026; interceptor drones the most-ordered category; UAH 33B (~$735M) ordered since launch; average delivery 8 days ([AeroTime](https://www.aerotime.aero/articles/ukraines-troops-order-590000-drones-interceptors-overtake-fpvs)).

- **Allied-funded:** VAMPIRE (US, 2022), Skynex (Germany, 2022–2024), TYTAN METIS (Germany for Ukraine).

## What it means for a US interceptor startup

- **Europe is the most active interceptor market outside Ukraine, but it buys European.** The realistic US-startup route is FMS through JIATF-401's pre-cleared marketplace (up to 25 nations) or a European production partner.
- **The Gulf buys both premium US systems (UAE $2.1B FS-LIDS, Kuwait $1.98B Anduril) and cheap Ukrainian ones.** A reusable US interceptor has to sit between them on price.
- **Allies are now buying off the US shelf.** "When they see the U.S. military buying certain products, they're all in" ([Ingraham](https://www.defenseone.com/business/2026/09/army-awards-more-4-billion-counterdrone-contracts-putting-pentagons-new-drone-marketplace-test/416327/)). A US award comes first, and export follows.
