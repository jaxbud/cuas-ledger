# Report 7 — Deep dive: what the government said, October 2025 to October 2026

*37 statements, rules, budgets and oversight findings, each linked to the primary or best available source. Quotes are kept short and exact. Where a source paraphrased, it's marked.*

## Five messages that came through every channel

1. **"Layered, no silver bullet."** BG Matt Ross (JIATF-401) said it at [the March 2026 industry day](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/printable/559708) and again with the [$7B IDIQ awards](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/09/army-awards-up-to-7b-in-sprawling-counter-drone-idiq/). CBO reached the same conclusion: [no single system gives full protection](https://ideas.repec.org/p/cbo/report/62262.html). For a new effector, the question buyers ask is which layer it fills and whether it plugs into the stack.
2. **Cost per kill is the yardstick.** "That puts us on the right end of the cost curve, and we will make that trade all day long" — Army Secretary Dan Driscoll on [$15K Merops against $30–50K Shaheds](https://www.armytimes.com/unmanned/2026/04/20/us-army-turns-to-ukraine-tested-drones-to-counter-iranian-uav-threat/), with [$3–5K the stated target at scale](https://www.twz.com/land/cheap-interceptor-drones-proven-in-ukraine-protected-u-s-troops-against-iranian-shaheds).
3. **Homeland defense is now its own mission.** Hegseth's [December 2025 guidance](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/01/pentagon-expands-task-forces-counter-drone-authorities-handing-commanders-more-flexibility/) lets commanders act beyond the fence line. NORTHCOM reports 1–2 incursions a day. The DoD IG found [major bases still uncovered](https://www.defensenews.com/unmanned/2026/01/28/bureaucratic-confusion-leaves-dod-sites-exposed-to-drones-dod-ig-says/). Ross: "We didn't want to wait for a 9/11 event inside the United States" ([Defense One](https://www.defenseone.com/threats/2026/03/drone-threat-will-far-exceed-gwots-roadside-bomb-threat-counter-drone-task-force-director/411921)).
4. **Buy fast, from a common shelf, on common standards.** The marketplace ([cuas.mil](https://www.defensenews.com/news/pentagon-congress/2026/08/10/pentagon-launches-online-marketplace-for-counter-uas-technology/)), Lattice as the C2, and the [US-UK data standard](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2026/03/mil-260312-arnews02.htm). "Vendors who adopt these standards will find a more direct path to their capabilities" (Ross).
5. **State and local police get authority, with guardrails.** SAFER SKIES (Dec 2025) and the [DOJ/DHS interim final rule](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-13609.pdf) (Jul 2026) create two certification tiers and an authorized-technology list expected to start RF-only.

## Reading the statements for Unified Mechanics

- **Language that helps UM:** "non-explosive kinetic interceptors for homeland," "low collateral," "sense in depth," "Ring doorbell" ease of setup, "entangling," and cost-curve pressure on missiles.
- **Language that's a warning:** "test against a common standard," "open architecture," "data standards," "no vendor lock-in." All of these favor products that are already characterized and integrated, and UM is neither yet.
- **The quiet but important one:** the state and local rule puts physical interception behind a list that doesn't yet include it.
