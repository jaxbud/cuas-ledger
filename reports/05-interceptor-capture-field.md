## What the record says about winning

1. **Combat proof wins first.** Merops and Sting were bought because they worked in Ukraine; VAMPIRE came back to the US Army after 350,000+ operational hours there.
2. **Test events are the gate.** Grizzly was picked after a common-criteria shoot-off. Fortem's Replicator 2 selection followed JIATF-401 evaluation.
3. **Price is public and falling.** $15K (Merops today), $3–5K (Merops at scale, per Driscoll), ~$2,500 (Ukrainian interceptors sold to the Gulf), <$250K (Army LCI, a different class).
4. **Speed matters more each year.** Merops 280 km/h; TYTAN METIS 400 km/h (~249 mph, above UM's 200 mph watch line); Skyhammer 700 km/h. NORTHCOM's 2024 view was that net drones can't chase 150–200+ mph FPVs.
5. **Homeland buyers prefer low collateral.** Fortem (capture) and Anvil (ram) are the domestic picks; Merops and BLAZE are explosive and mostly bought for overseas use.

## UM's best opening in this field

A recoverable interceptor with onboard radar and EO/IR that defeats RF-silent drones with capture first and a ram later. No contract to date has been awarded for exactly that combination. The closest analogues are Fortem (capture plus radar, fixed nest), Anvil (ram, Lattice-native) and Alpine Eagle (airborne sensing, European). The gap is real but narrow, and it can only be held with test data.
